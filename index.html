Every ticket routed a defined amount to the mission at the moment of purchase, the full revenue split was disclosed to every buyer — and buyers still chose to pay more. Seven in ten buyers upgraded — with the full split disclosed.

What it means. Disclosure of the exact split was present for every upgrade decision. Buyers paid more with full transparency, not in spite of hidden costs.
What it doesn't prove. Co-occurrence isn't causation. We can't yet say buyers paid more because of transparency — only that the two happened together. Measuring motive is the next step.

The Investor-Safe Logic

The proven chain, stated at the precision it earns.

1
Every ticket guaranteed the nonprofit its full base amount at the moment of purchase — the mission's revenue was never contingent on event performance.
2
With the complete revenue split disclosed at checkout, 70.37% of buyers chose the premium tier — between 57% and 81% at 95% confidence, versus a 20–30% industry benchmark.
3
Buyers demonstrated willingness to pay above the listed maximum: ~$205 in unprompted tips at checkout.
4
The event netted positive for the studio ($289.35) with zero marketing spend, while delivering the nonprofit a 52% effective share.
∴
Full transparency coexisted with category-leading conversion and above-sticker generosity. Buyers upgraded to fund the artist, the studio, or both — the disjunctive claim the data supports — and did so despite a fully disclosed studio share, not demonstrably because of it.
