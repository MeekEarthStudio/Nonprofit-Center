Step 2 · The practice

Methods to be more transparent

Six concrete practices, each drawn from a named source — a transparency guide should show its own receipts.

  1. Publish GAAP-compliant financial statements

    Follow ASC 958 (the nonprofit accounting standard), include real footnote disclosures, and commission an independent audit when scale warrants it. Post the results where donors can find them.

    Source: Gray, Gray & Gray, LLP — Best Practices for Transparency in Financial Reporting for Nonprofits.

  2. File the Form 990 on time, every year

    Know your filing threshold and deadline for the 2026 reporting cycle. Three consecutive missed filings trigger automatic revocation of tax-exempt status — staying current is transparency's floor.

    Source: Mission M — Nonprofit Transparency Checklist (2026 reporting cycle), citing the National Council of Nonprofits.

  3. Earn a Candid Seal of Transparency

    The 2026 Seal process is streamlined and free — and it's the practice with the clearest measured payoff (the 62% contribution lift above). Claim your profile, complete it, renew it annually.

    Source: Candid — Introducing the 2026 Seals of Transparency.

  4. Share dashboards and impact reports

    Publish an annual report and a living dashboard of program outcomes, and keep state charitable-registration filings current (requirements vary by state — NASCO maintains the map).

    Source: Zeffy — Best Practices for Nonprofit Financial Transparency (2026 Guide).

  5. Enforce a real conflict-of-interest policy

    Annual written disclosures from every board member, documented recusals when conflicts arise, and minutes that show it happened. Governance transparency is donor transparency.

    Source: Boardable — Nonprofit Board Best Practices in 2026.

  6. Disclose fees and fund flow at the point of donation

    Tell donors what the platform keeps, what the charity nets, and when the money arrives — before they give, not after. This is exactly where donor expectations are strictest.

    Source: Give.org Donor Trust Special Report (2026) — fee transparency 53%, accurate information 51%, ≤5% fees 46%, three-day delivery 62%.

    Meek Earth Studio PBC

    Meek Earth Studio is a public benefit corporation that produces benefit concerts with transparency built into the ticket itself. Our value to nonprofits is a revenue model any donor can hold in their head:
